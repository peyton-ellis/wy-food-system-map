Data sources

  • Food access, poverty, SNAP and vehicle access: USDA ERS, Food Access Research Atlas – SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map (July 2026 release). Uses 2020 census tracts and road (driving) distance to the nearest SNAP-authorized store. I recomputed county shares from the 160 tract records. Poverty rate and median family income are population-weighted averages of tract values.
  • Farm production, sales and crop acres: USDA NASS, 2022 Census of Agriculture County Profiles, one per county. The 23 county sales totals add up to the state total of $1.60 billion and 10,544 farms. "(D)" means NASS withheld the value to protect individual operations. Crop acres are available only for each county's five largest crops, so a blank does not mean zero. "Hay & other crop sales" includes sugarbeets.
  • Food insecurity, store counts, farmers markets, processors, composting, local sales and agritourism: the Wyoming Food Systems Map (wy.foodsystemsmap.org) export you supplied. Its underlying sources are Feeding America Map the Meal Gap (2021), the USDA Food Environment Atlas (store rates, 2016–2017), USDA AMS directories (2023) and the Census of Agriculture (2022).
  • School meals: Wyoming Department of Education, Fall enrollment K-12, USDA free and reduced lunch by school, 2025–26 (the file you supplied). It covers 337 public schools and 86,745 students. County rates are weighted by enrollment and use only schools in the National School Lunch Program. "N/R" (too few students to report) values are left out. "≥"/"≤" values are used at their bound. At schools using the Community Eligibility Provision (CEP), the reported eligibility rate is the claiming percentage for the school group (the identified student percentage times 1.6, capped at 100%). It is not an individual count, so CEP counties such as Goshen and Hot Springs can look higher than an application-based count would. Schools not in the federal program, all of Niobrara #1, Lincoln #1, Weston #7, Fremont #24, Park #16 and Sheridan #3 and some schools in Sheridan #1, Washakie #1 and Weston #1, have no eligibility data. The "outside the federal lunch program" map shows how much of each county that affects. Three statewide charter schools (Cheyenne Classical, Wyoming Classical, Prairie View) count only toward state totals.
  • School locations: schools are placed at the center of their town, assigned from district and school name, and spread slightly so they don't overlap. They show roughly where schools are, not street addresses. Rural schools appear at the nearest town. Prairie View Community School is not placed.
  • Farm to school: USDA Farm to School Census (2018–19) results, from the Wyoming Food Systems Map export.
  • UW Extension offices: the 27 offices listed at uwyo.edu/uwe/uwe-county-offices.html: one in each county, second offices in Fremont (Riverton), Lincoln (Kemmerer) and Park (Powell), and the Wind River Indian Reservation office. Stars are placed at the town center.
  • Sites: the point layer from the same Wyoming Food Systems Map export. Ten crop-processing records were geocoded outside the U.S. in the source file, so they are placed at the center of the town listed in their address. WFC Aggregation & Distribution grantees come from your notes and are placed at town level.

How to read it

  • Counties with only 1–3 census tracts (Niobrara, Crook, Hot Springs and others) can swing to 0% or 100% on the tract-flag indicators.
  • The combined score averages each county's percentile rank across the chosen need indicators: access, food insecurity, poverty, SNAP and store scarcity. Income and store counts are reversed so that higher always means more need. Production and context indicators are not included. The score compares counties with each other; it does not measure need in absolute terms.
  • Indicator years differ (2016–2023), so compare them with care.
  • Colors: each map splits counties, or tracts, into five bands with about the same number of areas in each (quintiles). The band holding the statewide value is outlined in the legend. Bands are relative to Wyoming, so a dark county is high compared with other Wyoming counties, not necessarily high nationally.
  • Map level: food access, poverty, income, SNAP and vehicle indicators can be shown by census tract. All other indicators are county-only.
  • Restaurants: © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (openstreetmap.org/copyright). Downloaded November 2026 (amenity = restaurant or fast_food; cafés and coffee shops not included). Chains are identified by brand tag and name. Fast-casual chains such as Chipotle, Qdoba and Panera count as fast-food chains. Unnamed and duplicate entries were removed, leaving 723 restaurants. OpenStreetMap is built by volunteers. It is fairly complete in larger towns and Jackson, but it misses places in rural counties: Converse shows 6 restaurants and Weston 1. Treat counts as a minimum, not a census.
  • Parks and reservation: National Park Service land is from U.S. Census Bureau area landmarks (2024), cut off at the Wyoming state line: Yellowstone, Grand Teton, the John D. Rockefeller Jr. Memorial Parkway, Bighorn Canyon, Devils Tower, Fossil Butte and Fort Laramie. Small units are circled so they can be seen. The Wind River Indian Reservation boundary is from the Census Bureau's American Indian Areas file (2024). Hover over an outline for its name.
  • Roads: U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line primary and secondary roads, 2024: interstates, U.S. highways, state highways and other major roads, simplified to about 200 m. Local and county roads are not shown.
  • Boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary files (2024 counties, 2020 census tracts), simplified for the web. Public domain.
